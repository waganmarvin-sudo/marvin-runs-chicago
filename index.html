My Story

I trained for months. The marathon still humbled me.

Last year I signed up for the Chicago Marathon with one goal: finish it, and finish it with a PR. I took training seriously — I had a plan and I followed it to a T. I woke up before sunrise, every single time, even when I was exhausted, because the goal was louder than the alarm clock. My endurance was the best it had ever been. My stamina felt unstoppable. When race week came, I knew I was ready.

Then race day happened.

At mile 10, severe cramps locked up my legs. I fought them as long as I could, but I had to call it. Ten miles into the race I had spent months preparing for — done. All those early mornings, and I was walking off the course instead of crossing the finish line. It was one of the most frustrating and disappointing days I've ever had as a runner.

But a DNF isn't the end of the story. It's the middle of it.

Here's the thing: I know exactly what went wrong, and I know exactly what to fix. I've spent the past year learning from that day — about fueling, about pacing, about myself. I'm training just as hard as last year, but this time I'm training smarter. This October, I'm going back to Chicago to finish what I started, and to chase that PR with everything I've learned.

And this time, I'm not just running for me. Every mile has a purpose bigger than a medal — clean water for kids who walk miles every day just to get water that could make them sick. I know what it's like to fall short of a goal. They shouldn't have to fall short of something as basic as water.

The Fun Part

Sponsor one of my 26.2 miles.

Here's the deal: pick a mile, and I run that mile for you. Donate any amount, dedicate the mile to anyone you want — yourself, your mom, your dog, someone you've lost — and on race day I'll carry that dedication with me through your mile. After the race, I'll email you a personal note (and a photo!) from your mile of the course.

Green miles are already claimed. Tap an open mile to claim it — many sponsors give $26.20 or $50, but every amount counts. Mile 26.2 — the finish line — is the big one. 😉

Claiming a mile opens two things: my official donate page, and a pre-written email to me so I know which mile is yours and who it's dedicated to. I update this board as claims come in.
